Why are you opening it now?

Respire is a free Android app that puts a breathing pause in front of the apps you open without thinking. Close them in one tap, or wait a few seconds and open them on purpose.

Google Play Download APK View the source code

Android 8 and later. Free and open source (MPL-2.0). No ads, no account, no Internet permission. Available on Google Play and GitHub.

Built from what research says works

Each feature rests on a study of smartphone overuse or on a stated design choice. The details, rule by rule, are on the page Why Respire works the way it does.

  • Pause before opening

    A breathing screen with a countdown and one question. Closing is available right away; opening only once the countdown ends.

    Friction before opening social apps cut opening attempts and made openings more intentional (Grüning, Riedel and Lorenz-Spreen, PNAS, 2023).

  • A wait that grows

    Each new attempt within the hour adds a few seconds. The more the urge comes back, the slower it gets.

  • Session end & “Just 2 min” choice

    Opening an app gives you 10 minutes (or just 2 minutes for a quick check). If you are still there, the pause comes back in the middle of the feed.

  • Daily limit

    After a set number of opens per app, the app stays closed until tomorrow. Extending a session counts as an open.

    Hard-to-override limits reduced social media use by about 16% over 12 weeks, and most people kept them (Allcott, Gentzkow and Song, NBER, 2021).

  • Risky hours

    Stricter rules during the hours you choose, for example in the evening. Respire suggests a time range from your own hourly data.

  • Reels and Shorts filter

    Leaves Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts as soon as they open. Messages, search and regular videos stay available.

  • A lock on loosening

    Removing an app or relaxing a rule takes a 30-second wait, so you don't undo your settings on a whim.

    People tend to pause these tools and relapse quickly (Haliburton et al., CHI, 2024).

  • Your week & home screen widget

    Pauses, estimated minutes saved and how often you gave up, by day, by hour, by app and on a discreet home screen widget, to see when temptation is strongest.

Everything stays on your phone

Respire has no Internet permission, so it cannot send anything anywhere, even by mistake.

  • Pauses only use the name of the app in the foreground.
  • The Reels and Shorts filter, off by default, only checks technical element IDs inside Instagram, YouTube and Facebook. It never reads text, messages or images.
  • Settings and counters stay in the app's private storage and disappear when you uninstall it.

Why Respire is free

I spend too much time scrolling endless feeds myself, and people close to me have needed the same help. Apps that really help do exist, but their most useful features are often behind a paywall: daily limits, blocking Reels or Shorts, statistics. Respire aims to make these tools available to everyone, with no subscription, no ads and no data collection. Someone who wants to take back control of their time should not have to pay for it.

Install

  1. Install from Google Play via the Google Play Store, or download Respire.apk on your phone (Android will ask you to allow installs from your browser).
  2. Choose the apps to slow down in Respire.
  3. Turn on Respire in Accessibility settings, from the button in the app.
  4. Android 13 and later: if the switch is greyed out, go to Settings, Apps, Respire, the ⋮ menu, then Allow restricted settings, and try again.

Questions

Why does Respire need accessibility access?

It is the only way on Android to know which app is opening, so the pause can appear before it. Respire uses it for nothing else.

Can I turn it off?

Yes, at any time in Accessibility settings or by uninstalling the app. The 30-second lock only applies to Respire's own rules.

Does it work on iPhone?

No. iOS doesn't allow this kind of app outside Apple's Screen Time framework.

How can I report a problem?

Open an issue on GitHub or write to pilmil.studio@proton.me. Respire collects no data, so your feedback is the only way to know what to improve.
